# PLA (Polylactic Acid)

Common, easy-to-print thermoplastic derived from renewable resources like corn starch.

## Key Properties
- **Type**: Biodegradable Thermoplastic
- **Printing Temp**: 190°C – 220°C
- **Bed Temp**: 50°C – 60°C (can print on cold tape)
- **Cooling**: 100% Fan (essential for details/bridges)
- **Enclosure**: Not required (open air is better)
- **Glass Transition (Tg)**: ~60°C (becomes soft)

## Pros
- Easiest material to print
- Minimal warping/shrinkage
- Sharp details and overhangs
- Rigid and strong (but brittle)
- No fumes (sweet smell)

## Cons
- Low heat resistance (deforms in hot cars/sun)
- Brittle (snaps rather than bending)
- Sensitive to moisture over time (becomes brittle)
- Hard to sand/post-process (melts)

## Design Guidelines
1.  **Snap-fits**: Use with caution. PLA will creep (relax) over time and lose tension. It is prone to snapping if bent too far.
2.  **Structural**: Good for static loads (compression), bad for impact or high tension.
3.  **Fillets**: Always fillet internal corners to reduce stress concentration.
4.  **Tolerances**: Excellent. Can achieve ±0.1mm accuracy on tuned printers.
5.  **Threads**: Prints widely used threads (M3+) very well.

## Typical Applications
- Decorative models
- Prototypes
- Enclosures for low-heat electronics
- Drawer organizers
- Jigs and fixtures (low heat)
